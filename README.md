# Noise-Filtering-Microphone-System
Noise Filtering Microphone System – EN2091 Analog Project

As part of the EN2091 Analog Electronics Project during my studies in Electronic & Telecommunication Engineering at the University of Moratuwa, I collaborated on the design and development of an analog Noise Filtering Microphone System. The project aimed to improve speech clarity by filtering out unwanted low- and high-frequency noise while amplifying the desired voice signal.

The system follows a complete analog signal-processing architecture consisting of a transistor-based preamplifier, an operational amplifier buffer, cascaded fourth-order Sallen-Key high-pass and low-pass filters, and a Class AB push-pull power amplifier with the dual power supply splitter circuit. The filters establish a speech frequency passband of 100 Hz to 5 kHz, attenuating unwanted frequency components and improving voice clarity.

A 24 V DC power supply with a custom rail-splitter circuit generates the required +12 V and −12 V dual supply rails for the analog circuitry. The complete circuit was simulated and analysed using LTspice, followed by custom two-layer PCB design and routing using Altium Designer. Additionally, the PCB and system enclosures were designed in SolidWorks, combining electronic circuit design with practical mechanical engineering.

Key contributions included analog circuit design and analysis, component selection, LTspice simulations, PCB design and hardware development, as well as system integration and testing.

This project strengthened my knowledge of analog electronics, active filter design, audio signal processing, power amplifier circuits, PCB development and hardware prototyping. It also provided valuable hands-on experience in translating theoretical electronic engineering concepts into a functional hardware solution.

Working together enhanced our technical knowledge, problem-solving capabilities and teamwork while providing practical experience in collaborative engineering and project development
